# ArtGeneration.me user guide

This guide covers everything the service can do: how to create images, video
and sound, how the AI chat and the editor work, what coins are spent on, and
what to do when the result is not what you had in mind.

![The "About" page](/assets/docs-pages/screens/01-01-about-page.en.webp)

The service itself is access to hundreds of neural networks at once, in one
browser window. Nothing to install, configure or update: you describe what you
want in words, pick a model, and it does the work. What comes out stays in your
profile — download it, show it in the public feed, or, on a paid plan, keep it
to yourself.

All of it is **one account and one shared wallet**: pictures, clips, sound, chat
and the editor spend the same coins, and there are no separate subscriptions
for individual skills.

<Callout type="tip">
  There is a second way in for a newcomer — the **"About"** page in the "More"
  menu. That is the shot above: five launch buttons (image, video, audio,
  AI chat, model catalog), and from there a **"next"** button at the bottom of
  each screen walks you through the sections — no manual scrolling needed. At
  the very bottom it carries its own list of common questions.

  This guide does not retell it; it explains in detail what is named there
  in a single line.
</Callout>

## Why this guide is worth reading

ArtGeneration is an online service, an ordinary website really: open it and work.
But behind that simplicity is a large project that has been **gathering layers
for years**: an image generator, then video, then sound, then the AI chat,
the editor, LoRAs, styles, masks, references, upscalers. Every layer brought its
own possibilities and its own quirks — and in the interface they sit side by side
while switching on in different ways.

We tried to make it all clear without a manual and put hints wherever they fit —
and still we **recommend leafing through this guide from end to end**. Not for
formality's sake: half the power here hides in places you won't look into until
you know something is there. A button that writes a prompt from your picture.
Mask inversion that saves half an hour of work. Searching by colour. Dragging
works with the mouse instead of "download and upload back".

And one more thing worth saying up front: **it is perfectly normal if the first
attempt looks nothing like a masterpiece**. That is how it should be. Neural
networks are a tool like a camera or a graphics editor: the result comes not from
the first button but from understanding what you are turning and why.

This is also where the most common disappointment comes from. The site looks like
a site, someone pokes around at random, types "a beautiful girl", gets something
mediocre — and concludes the service is weak. Almost always it is neither
the service nor the model: the prompt gave it nothing to hold on to, the model
was not the one for the job, and half the tools went unnoticed. The difference
between "meh" and "excellent" is usually a couple of paragraphs of reading,
not a different neural network.

<Callout type="tip">
  If you have already tried and were unimpressed — start with
  [how to write prompts](/en/docs/generator). It is the fastest return on five
  minutes anywhere in this guide.
</Callout>

If you don't want to read all of it — read at least
[Generator](/en/docs/generator) and [Coins and limits](/en/docs/coins).
Those two pages answer most of what people write to support about.

## Where to start

The order that saves a newcomer the most time:

1. **Look through the [gallery](/en/docs/gallery)** — the best way to see what
   is possible at all. Any work's prompt can be taken with one click, and which
   model made it and with what settings is in the "Details" block — it is
   collapsed, which is why few people know about it.
2. **[Sign in](/en/docs/start)** — without signing in you can only browse,
   search included. There is no separate registration: the first sign-in creates
   the account, and no password is needed — a code by email or a social network.
   The first sign-in comes with a gift.
3. **[Claim your daily coins](/en/docs/coins)** — they are not credited
   automatically; the header has a **"Забрать"** ("Claim") button for that.
   This is the first thing everyone trips over.
4. **[Make your first picture](/en/docs/generator)** — one phrase and one button.

<Callout type="tip">
  Not sure which model to start with — take one tagged **HIT**. That is not
  an editorial pick but what everyone else is using most right now, and the set
  changes on its own.
</Callout>

## Five things worth trying on the very first evening

Not for the sake of it — after these the service makes more sense than after
any amount of reading:

1. **Scribble with the mouse and turn it into a picture.** The "Custom Image"
   block has a brush: a crooked stick-figure house becomes a finished
   illustration with the composition preserved. How it works is in
   [Generator](/en/docs/generator).
2. **Ask the service what is in a picture.** The magnifier button on the preview
   of an uploaded image writes a detailed prompt for you — a great way to take
   apart someone else's trick. Details in
   [Special features](/en/docs/special).
3. **Replace one object in a finished frame using a mask.** Paint over the area,
   write what should be there, and nothing else is touched.
4. **Send your picture to the [AI chat](/en/docs/chat)** and ask what to improve.
   Models tagged **VISION** really do look at the image.
5. **Bring a picture to life as video.** Above a finished result in the generator
   there is a button labelled **«Анимировать (image → video)»** — the label is not
   translated — which puts your picture in as the first frame and switches to
   the "Video" tab. See [Generator](/en/docs/generator).

<Callout type="tip">
  Works can be **dragged with the mouse** from your feed on the left straight
  into the "Custom Image" block on the right. No downloading and re-uploading —
  that is the most common piece of wasted work for newcomers. How it works is
  in [Generator](/en/docs/generator).
</Callout>

## How this guide is built

Every feature here follows the same order: **why** you need it, **how it works**,
an **example** and a **tip**. If something in the service behaves unexpectedly,
there is most likely a paragraph about exactly that.

Exact numbers — prices, limits, package sizes — are deliberately not repeated
in the text. They change, and the screen always has the current ones. Instead
the guide says **where on the screen to look**: what each plan includes, for
instance, is always listed in the plans window, which opens by clicking your
balance in the header.

<Callout type="tip">
  Looking for the answer to a specific question — **use the search on the left**.
  It searches the text of the pages, not just headings, and forgives typos.
</Callout>

And to get back here from the service, look for the word **"Вики"** ("Wiki") —
that is what the guide is called both in the user menu and in the "More" menu.
