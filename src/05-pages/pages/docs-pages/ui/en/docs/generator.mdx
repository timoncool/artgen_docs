# Generator

The generator is the main screen of the service. Images, video and sound are made
here. Your works are on the left, the canvas and the prompt field in the middle,
the settings of the chosen model on the right.

![Generator, Images tab](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-01-generator-images.en.webp)

## Three tabs at the top

**Images · Video · Audio** — three different sets of models and three different
sets of settings. Switching the tab changes the whole right-hand column.

<Callout type="warning">
  The generator **remembers the tab between visits**. If you opened it and saw
  Video instead of images, that is not a bug — you were working with video
  last time.
</Callout>

Until something is created, an empty placeholder sits where the canvas is —
that is not a loading error.

## How to write prompts

The prompt field carries a hint: **"What do you want to create? Write a prompt
in Russian or English, or click the dice for a random prompt"**.

<Callout type="tip">
  **Plain language works almost everywhere.** The vast majority of models are
  marked as accepting prompt translation, so you rarely need to translate
  anything yourself.
</Callout>

### What is actually worth writing

The model does not know what you mean. "A beautiful girl" is not a prompt,
it is a topic: millions of different pictures fit it, and you will get a random
one. Concrete words give it something to hold on to, and usually four things
are enough:

1. **Who or what is in the frame** — "a red fox", "a wooden house", "a woman
   in a knitted sweater".
2. **Where and when** — "in a snowy forest", "in a kitchen in the morning",
   "against a neon sign".
3. **How it is shot or drawn** — "photorealistic", "watercolour", "a film still",
   "a flat icon".
4. **Light and mood** — "soft morning light", "backlit sunset", "a warm cosy
   evening".

**Example.** Instead of "a winter house" — "a cosy wooden cabin with a red roof,
a fir tree beside it, snow on the ground, warm light in the window, soft winter
evening sky, storybook illustration". The second phrase gives the model
everything it needs so it does not have to guess.

<Callout type="tip">
  **Don't put negations in the main prompt.** "No people", "not cartoonish" often
  work backwards: the model sees the words "people" and "cartoonish". There is
  a separate **"Negative"** field for that — write there what should not be
  in the picture.
</Callout>

### Three buttons that write for you

- **The dice** next to the field inserts a random ready-made prompt. A great way
  to see what a "good" prompt even looks like and take it apart.
- **The sparkles ("Improve prompt")** — this is **the service's own improvement**.
  It fires on click and works with any model: the service takes your short phrase
  and rewrites it into a detailed one. A notification "Improving the prompt"
  appears, and the updated text lands in the field — from there you can edit it
  by hand.
- **The microphone** — dictate the prompt out loud. The shortcut is
  **Ctrl + Shift + M** (⌘ + Shift + M on a Mac); it stops the recording too.

![Tooltips of the buttons near the prompt field](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-37-prompt-buttons-tooltips.en.webp)

While recording, the button turns red and shows an equaliser reacting to your
voice, with a countdown beside it: **the recording is capped at one minute**.
After you stop, the text is recognised, lands in the field, and the field flashes.

![Recording in progress: the equaliser and the countdown to one minute](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-79-voice-recording.en.webp)

Voice input is available on paid plans and spends coins like an ordinary
generation. If nothing could be recognised, the coins come back.

If the recording does not start at all, it is almost always the browser
permission. The first time it asks by itself, and that question needs a "yes";
if you have already refused, use the lock icon left of the address → site
settings → microphone.

![The browser asking for microphone permission](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-80-mic-permission.en.webp)

The button has its own hint for every case — access denied, no microphone
found, microphone busy with another program. And "we heard nothing" is not
an error at all: the button returns to its normal state by itself after
a couple of seconds.

There is a fourth route: **upload a picture and press
the magnifier button on its preview** — the service writes the prompt from
the picture itself. Details in [Special features](/en/docs/special).

<Callout type="warning">
  **Don't confuse it with "Prompt extend".** The sparkles button is the service's
  improvement, on demand. The **"Prompt extend"** toggle in the "Additional" block
  is a setting of **the model itself**: it exists not on every model, and where
  it does it may be on by default. Then the model reworks the wording on its own
  side, and you will not see that in the prompt field.

  If you know exactly what you want and the result keeps drifting away from it —
  check that toggle and try switching it off.
</Callout>

### How much text fits

Next to the price you can see how many characters still fit. The limits differ
between models, and some models have none at all — then the counter says so.
**Both states are normal.**

<Callout type="warning">
  Prompt length counts **more than your own words**: the prompts of selected
  styles and the trigger words of selected LoRAs are added to it. For video
  and sound only your own text is counted.
</Callout>

## Choosing a model

Under the tabs there is a model search and **coloured category tags**: NEW, HIT,
ART, PHOTO, ANIME, 3D, DESIGN, RU TEXT and others, plus a "More …" button
that reveals the rest.

Tags work as a filter. Within one group they act as "or", different groups
combine as "and".

![The full list of model tags](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-14-all-model-tags.en.webp)

<Callout type="tip">
  Tags come in two kinds, and the colour shows which: **coloured ones
  are categories** (what the model does: photo, anime, design, editing),
  **grey ones are the provider and the families** (SDXL, FLUX, Pony, Recraft
  and dozens more). So **you can filter models by family right in the generator**.
</Callout>

- **NEW** — the model appeared recently; the tag drops off by itself
  after about a month.
- **HIT** — the model is currently among the most used; the set changes on its own.

Then comes the model carousel. A card shows the **price per generation**,
the tags, and — for models above your plan — a **plan badge**.

<Callout type="tip">
  **The coloured dot to the left of the model name shows how busy it is right
  now**: green — good availability, yellow — moderate, red — poor. An
  inconspicuous detail that saves time: a red dot means a longer wait, and it is
  wiser to take a neighbouring model of the same family — the result will be
  similar.
</Callout>

Another hint appears **above the model search** when the selected model is
missing something: "The selected model requires a 'Custom Image'" or "…requires
a 'Custom Image' and a mask". It comes **before** you launch, not after
a refusal — and it settles half of the "why won't it generate" questions.

<Callout type="tip">
  The plan badge is shown **only if the model is above your plan**.
  A missing badge is good news: the model is available to you.
</Callout>

The list icon next to the "Model" heading opens a **panel with all models
in a grid** — with its own search, the same filters and bookmark stars.

![Model picker panel](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-24-model-panel-list.en.webp)

<Callout type="tip">
  The carousel shows two or three models, the panel shows a dozen at once.
  When picking among unfamiliar ones, open the panel: comparing prices and tags
  there is much faster. And to understand how models differ in substance, look
  into [Model catalogs](/en/docs/catalogs).
</Callout>

## Why models have different settings

The most common question sounds like this: "this model has no Negative field,
where did it go?"

The answer: **settings are not universal**. Some blocks are switched on by
the model itself, others come from its own schema. If a block is missing,
the model does not support it — the interface is not broken.

<Callout type="tip">
  This is the norm, not the exception: roughly every third model has no
  creativity mode, every fourth has no seed field, every fourth has no negative
  prompt.
</Callout>

**A hidden block is not cosmetics, it is a ban.** The server rejects
a generation if you send a value for a setting the model does not have.
So "this model does not have that setting" is precisely true: it will not be
ignored, it will be refused. And the other way round — **if the block is
visible, it definitely works**.

**Tip.** Don't hunt for a setting — change the model: the set of blocks changes
the moment you pick a new one.

### Layers: how the blocks of the right panel differ

Blocks in the right panel come in two kinds, and the difference matters.

- **A plain block** — a title, a hint and content. It cannot be collapsed
  or switched off: that is how the model, creativity mode, resolution, duration
  and FPS work.
- **A layer** — the same **plus a stack icon on the left and a chevron on
  the right**. That is your own image, the schema's media fields, "Additional",
  styles, LoRAs, and video frames and references.

A layer has **two independent states**: on or off (the stack icon) and expanded
or collapsed (the chevron).

<Callout type="warning">
  **A switched-off layer does not take part in the generation but stays in
  place** — it does not disappear, it fades. That is fundamentally different from
  "the model has no such block": there it truly does not exist, here you switched
  it off yourself.

  This is exactly what explains the error **"The 'Custom Image' layer is switched
  off"**: the picture is loaded, but the layer is dimmed, and the model won't
  get it.
</Callout>

**Dragging is smarter than it looks: drag a work onto a collapsed or
switched-off layer and it will expand and switch itself on** to accept
the file. No need to unfold the block first — just bring the picture to it.

A layer is a switch, not a delete: the settings inside are preserved. Built
a frame with a reference and want to see "how it looks without it" — don't
remove the picture, dim the layer and run again.

If a block has no stack icon, it cannot be switched off, and looking for
a toggle is pointless. Switching layers off is available on paid plans: on
the free one, clicking the icon opens the plans window.

## Resolution

Resolution is set in two steps: first a **quality preset** — every model has its
own set, from 0.5K up to 4K — then **proportions** with a slider. While you drag
the slider, the exact size in pixels shows above it. Switching the preset keeps
the chosen proportions, and the price is recalculated immediately next to it.

![The Resolution block: presets, the proportions slider and the exact size](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-19-resolution-block.en.webp)

Video has its own presets instead — from 480p up to 4K, a different set on every
model; the preset row only appears at all if a model has more than one. **Duration**
appears there too.

<Callout type="tip">
  **Choose the shape of the frame first, then raise the quality.** When you switch
  presets the service looks for the closest aspect ratio in the new one, so
  a vertical frame stays vertical — the shape will not slip.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  **The set of sizes changes when a custom image is loaded.** On some models
  the list narrows to a fixed one, and it looks as if the options vanished.
  Remove the picture and the list comes back. Hence the trick: if the proportions
  you need are missing — take the custom image off, choose the size, then put
  the picture back.

  And on video models that take the size from the source, the proportions slider
  goes dark altogether: the aspect ratio is read from the file. The quality
  presets keep working.
</Callout>

Next to it sits **the number of images per run**. How many options there are is
decided by the model; which of them are available to you, by your plan.
Unavailable ones are not hidden but shown inactive, and clicking such a number
opens the plans window.

<Callout type="warning">
  **The price is multiplied by the count**: four pictures cost four times as much.
  Try an unfamiliar model on one, and raise the count once the prompt has settled —
  that way you pay for exploring variants, not for exploring wordings.
</Callout>

## Your own image

A file can be dragged from disk **or straight from your own feed on the left** —
no need to download and re-upload. The drop zone lights up with a dashed border
when what you are dragging fits; it ignores the wrong file type, and that
is not an error.

The number of slots is set by the model and written right above the frame:
"Available to upload: …". When the slots are full, the drop zone **disappears
completely** — it does not go grey, it goes away. **Resolution adapts
to the first file automatically.**

![Custom image loaded](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-60-img2img-sdxl-strength.en.webp)

The preview itself carries four buttons in its corners, each with a hint:
**crop**, **remove image**, **extract prompt from image** and **edit image** —
the last one opens the uploaded file in the [editor](/en/docs/editor).

![The "Crop image" hint on the preview](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-82-custom-image-crop.en.webp)

![The "Edit image" hint on the preview](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-81-custom-image-edit.en.webp)

Once the file is in place, **"Draw mask"**, **"Remove background"** and
**"Modification strength"** appear. The last one decides how far the model
departs from the source: the scale has labelled notches — **Minimal / Medium /
Maximal**. To repaint an object while keeping the angle and background, stay
closer to minimal; for a new scene inspired by the source, closer to maximal.
The slider is not on every model, and its range is set by the model.

If the picture is not yet saved among your works, it is saved automatically
before the background is removed — leaving you with **two works**: the source and
the result. Those are not duplicates.

On models that require a mask, the drawing window opens by itself — once, right
after the upload. Close it without saving and it will not come back on its own;
after that only the "Draw mask" button reopens it.

<Callout type="tip">
  Uploading a personal photo — switch **private mode** on yourself, with
  the toggle in the right panel: it does not come on by itself. Then the work
  will not go into the public feed. Private mode is available on a paid plan.
</Callout>

### Four buttons on the preview itself

| Where | What it is |
|---|---|
| top left | **"Crop image"** — crop again, after uploading |
| top right | **"Remove image"** |
| bottom left | **"Extract prompt from image"** — the service writes the prompt for you |
| bottom right | **"Edit image"** — opens the [editor](/en/docs/editor) right from here |

<Callout type="tip">
  The top-left button **changes meaning**: if the picture was uploaded as a file,
  it crops; if you drew it, the same button takes you back into the drawing
  window to finish it.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  **To swap the reference you don't need the bin — just drop another work onto
  the preview.** But note: a drop onto an occupied slot replaces the file rather
  than adding a second one, and the **painted mask is wiped along with the old
  picture** — it was tied to that image. The mask has to be drawn again.
</Callout>

**Cropping is not "trimming the file" but a required step.** The window is
named accordingly: "Set the visible area to use the image for generation".
On some models it pops up by itself right after the upload.

And here is the main thing: **until the visible area is set, the generation
will not start**. Close the window without pressing "Save" and the block shows
an explanation with a **"Set visible area"** button instead of settings. This
is the single most common cause of "the Create button doesn't work": the person
is sure they just closed a window they did not need.

The frame is tied to the chosen resolution rather than free: change the size
and the frame changes with it.

If the block shows an explanation instead of an upload area, there are only four
reasons: **your plan does not allow it** (your plan will be named in the text),
**the model does not allow it**, **both at once** — or exactly **the visible area
is not set**. The first three are refusals; the last is an unfinished step.

### Draw instead of uploading

Next to the upload button there is a **brush**. It opens a window
**"Draw your own image"** — a clean canvas where you can sketch the composition
by hand. Below the canvas: brush size, a colour field with an eyedropper,
a brush / eraser toggle, clear, and the resolution of the future picture.

![Drawing your own image](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-62-draw-own-image.en.webp)

**Example.** On the left of the shot is a sketch drawn with a mouse in a minute:
a box for the house, a triangle for the roof, a fir tree, a ground line.
Here is what came out of it:

![Result from the sketch](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-63-sketch-to-image-result.en.webp)

The composition survived intact: house in the centre, fir tree on the left,
roof of the same shape. Everything else — light, snow, wood grain, smoke from
the chimney — the model filled in.

<Callout type="warning">
  **A sketch alone is not enough — the model matters.** On a photorealistic model
  a line sketch stays a line sketch, even with "Modification strength" turned up.
  This worked on a model tagged **EDITOR**, with the prompt written as
  an instruction: "turn this line sketch into a warm storybook illustration…".
  If the result stubbornly resembles your drawing, change the model, not
  the slider.
</Callout>

### Mask: show exactly what to change

If the model supports it, **"Draw mask"** appears under the preview. The window
says that the painted area will be changed during generation.

![Mask editor](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-61-mask-dialog.en.webp)

There are three tools: brush size, a brush / eraser toggle and **invert**.

<Callout type="tip">
  **Invert saves the most time.** Want to keep the character and replace
  everything around them — paint over the character (that is quick) and hit
  invert. No need to paint the whole background by hand.
</Callout>

On some models the button is replaced by "Mask is not available for the selected
model", and on erasing models — the opposite: "A mask is required for the
selected model". That is a property of the model, not a fault.

### If the file did not upload

| What is wrong | What the service shows |
|---|---|
| wrong format | "Unsupported file format. Use jpeg, png or webp" |
| file too heavy | "The file is too large. Maximum size is 20 MB" |
| file too light | "The file is too small and cannot be uploaded" |
| file unreadable | "Could not read the file. Try another image…" |
| too many files at once | "You selected too many files" |

## Styles, LoRAs and additional settings

![Additional settings](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-25-additional-settings.en.webp)

Below come the blocks. **Your own image, styles, LoRAs and "Additional"** can be
collapsed and switched off — they carry the stack icon. Private mode, "Noise"
and the image count are plain blocks: you can only fill them in. Prompt extend
is not a block of its own but a toggle **inside** "Additional", and not on every
model.

<Callout type="tip">
  **You can pick no more than three styles and three LoRAs.** The trigger words
  of selected LoRAs are appended to the prompt automatically — you don't type
  them by hand, but they do eat into the prompt length.
</Callout>

A selected style or LoRA has **a toggle and a cross, and they are different
things**: the toggle switches it off temporarily without losing it from the set,
the cross removes it altogether. Handy for comparing "with and without" instead
of hunting for the style in the ribbon again.

<Callout type="tip">
  **The five icons above the styles ribbon are not a filter but a change of
  "sitter".** Cat, apple, tractor, man, woman: the previews of **all** styles are
  redrawn on the chosen subject. An inconspicuous control that decides a lot:
  element of the block — a style that looks great on a cat can fall apart
  on a portrait.

  Planning a portrait — switch the sitter to a person first, and only then pick
  a style.
</Callout>

**A style is not a black box: its card shows the prompt itself, and you can
copy it** (by pressing the heading, not by selecting the text). The style's
"Avoid" is there too, if it has one.

Hence the best trick of the whole topic: open the prompt of a style you like
and take its wording into your own. It works even where the style itself is
above your plan.

**The order of styles matters.** They do not blend; they stack in turn, each
one wrapping the result of the previous. And your text may end up **inside**
the style's template rather than before it — which is why short wordings work
better with styles. Write the substance; the style supplies the framing.

**Three ways to get "nothing happens" with a LoRA**, and all three are silent:

1. **three are already chosen** — a click on the fourth simply does not fire,
   with no message at all. Remove one first;
2. **the LoRA is above your plan** — the card is covered by a gradient with
   a large padlock, and a click opens the plans window instead of selecting it;
3. **the whole LoRA layer is switched off** — the LoRAs are
   chosen and visible, yet they never reach the generation. The same goes for
   individual toggles inside the panel.

**What happens to LoRAs when you change the model** is the commonest "bug" that
is not a bug: incompatible ones disappear **silently**, with no message; the
strength you set survives only if it falls inside the new model's range,
otherwise it reverts to the author's default; a LoRA switched off with the toggle
stays off; anything beyond three is dropped. Compatibility is not decided by
the family name: **if a LoRA is visible in the list under your model, it fits**.

**Video has no LoRAs at all** — not a single video model accepts them.

The list of LoRAs **is assembled for the selected model**, and each one's
weight is already set to the value its author recommends. Don't turn the
strength blindly and don't stack three at once — they almost always fight each
other; switch them on one at a time with the toggle. An empty LoRA list is not
a connection failure but a property of the model: it does not support them.
More in [Model catalogs](/en/docs/catalogs).

<Callout type="warning">
  **"Noise" does not guarantee a repeat if prompt extension is on.** The setting's
  own description says as much: it affects reproducibility. To repeat a work
  exactly, set "Noise" **and** switch prompt extension off in "Additional".
  The seed alone is not enough.
</Callout>

Three of the common ones are worth knowing. **"Prompt adherence"** — how strictly
to follow the text: the slider has labelled notches from "Low" to "Strict".
**"Noise"** is the seed, the number that lets you repeat a frame; a green dice
beside it means the value is random. And **"Grain"** is about texture, not
focus: it makes the picture more textured, but it will not rescue a blurry frame.

The set of additional settings **comes with the model itself**, and every model
has its own. SDXL models bring "Prompt Adherence", which newer models do not
have; design models bring a colour palette and a background colour; some models
bring "Prompt extend". "Creativity mode" is not from "Additional" at all — it is
a separate block of the right panel, right under the model. It has three steps —
**Sketch, Draft, Masterpiece** — and the captions at the ends of the slider say
plainly what you pay with: "Faster" on the left, "Higher quality" on the right.

![Creativity mode: Sketch, Draft, Masterpiece](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-78-creativity-mode.en.webp)

<Callout type="tip">
  The practical takeaway: **changed the model — scroll through "Additional"
  again**. A setting you were used to may be gone, and a new one may have
  appeared.
</Callout>

## Your own feed on the left and dragging

The arrow button at the left edge opens a **drawer with your works**: search,
grid density and media type switches. From there a work can be dragged straight
into the "your image" slot.

![Left drawer with your works](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-54-left-drawer-works.en.webp)

<Callout type="tip">
  The shot also shows a deleted work — red, with a bin and a restore arrow.
  Until you leave the page it can be brought back with one click. More
  in [Your works](/en/docs/my-works).
</Callout>

**Hover a card in the drawer and an enlarged preview appears beside it, with
the resolution, the date and the first line of the prompt on top.** It is
the fastest way to find the right work among a hundred thumbnails — no need
to open a card and come back. Video plays silently in the preview and audio
gets a waveform player, so a track can be **listened to without opening it**.

The drawer has its own search — it looks **through the words of your prompt** and
takes the first ten words into account. Search by one or two rare words: a long
phrase gets truncated anyway. It also has its own density slider: a single column
turns the drawer into a large preview feed, which is the most convenient way
to pick.

The right panel can be collapsed too — then only the canvas and the prompt
field remain.

![Collapsed right panel](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-13-right-panel-collapsed.en.webp)

### On a phone this works differently

The drawers open with **a swipe from the edge of the screen** — an invisible trap
strip runs along the very edge. A short, brisk movement is enough; there is no
need to drag all the way.

<Callout type="warning">
  **Only one drawer is open at a time.** Open your works and the settings close
  by themselves — and the tab of the second drawer **is not shown at all**. Hence
  "the settings panel disappeared on my phone": it did not, just close the drawer
  with your works.
</Callout>

Two lesser-known tricks. First: **the tab can be dragged up and down
with a finger**, and it stays where you left it — separately for the left and
the right drawer, and it survives a reload. That saves you when the tab covers
the button you need. Second: at the bottom of an open drawer there is a wide
**"Close gallery"** button — if swipes are not your thing, close it with that.

Also on a phone the improve-prompt button moves out of the input field into
the bottom bar, the Ctrl + Enter hint is not shown, and the create button itself
becomes round with an upward arrow.

## Launching and waiting

The launch shortcut is **Ctrl + Enter** (⌘ + Enter on Mac) and works anywhere
on the page. On a phone the hint is not shown and the button is round,
with an upward arrow.

![Generation in progress](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-55-generation-in-progress.en.webp)

While the work is being made, a notification hangs at the top and a placeholder
with a spinner sits in the feed on the left. You don't need to leave the page,
but you don't need to watch it either: the result appears by itself.

Works run in a queue, so time may pass between the press and the result —
that is not a freeze.

**Read the error text straight away.** Finished rows disappear from the panel
after about a minute, and then there is nowhere to look up the cause — hence
the eternal "something red flashed and I missed it". The work itself does not
go anywhere: a failed attempt stays in the feed as a cross in a circle.

A generation also has a time limit: if the network never answers, the job closes
itself with **"The generation time has expired"** — and **the coins come back**.
Slow models wait longer, and that is normal.

**If several pictures were ordered, a row of thumbnails appears under the big
one** — for switching between variants of the same batch. With a single picture
there is no row. A finished result **can be dragged straight from the canvas**
into the settings fields, just like from the left drawer. That works for images
and sound; video cannot be dragged.

## What you can do with a finished work

As soon as the work is ready, a row of buttons appears above it — and this is
the most useful place in the generator. The buttons form three meaningful
groups.

![A finished result and the action buttons](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-50-generation-result.en.webp)

The buttons are icons only, but each has a tooltip on hover — and the tooltip is
what explains them. Here they are in order, left to right.

### Send to "Custom Image"

Puts the finished result into the source slot, so the next generation starts
from it. That is how a chain is built: generate, send to the source, rewrite
the prompt, get a development of the same picture.

![The "Send to Custom Image" hint](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-50b-send-to-custom-image.en.webp)

The button is smarter than it looks, because the source size has to suit
the model. If the result matches the current size, the picture is simply
placed in. If the model has that size among its own, **the resolution switches
by itself**. And if there is no suitable one, the cropper opens — otherwise
the model would not take the file. The canvas is cleared, and the "Custom Image"
layer switches on and unfolds on its own: a clean field for the next step.

### Create a similar image

Runs **the very same prompt with a random "Noise" value** — and makes exactly
one picture, however many were set in the settings. It is the cheapest way
to get another take on the same idea: nothing needs to be changed.

![The "Create a similar image" hint](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-50c-create-similar.en.webp)

That is why the result differs every time: the seed is reset to random. If you
need an exact repeat rather than another take, set "Noise" by hand
in the additional settings.

### Animate (image to video)

Switches the generator to the **"Video"** tab, puts your picture in as
**the first frame** and unfolds the frames block by itself. What is left
is to pick a video model and write what should happen in the shot.

![The "Animate (image to video)" hint](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-50d-animate-to-video.en.webp)

The prompt is not carried over: video has its own logic of description —
there you write about movement, not about the content of the frame.

### Discuss with AI in chat

Uploads the picture as an attachment and opens the [AI chat](/en/docs/chat)
with it. While the file is uploading, a spinner runs in place of the icon —
that is a couple of seconds, not a freeze. If the upload fails, a notification
says so and nothing is lost.

![The "Discuss with AI in chat" hint](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-50e-discuss-in-chat.en.webp)

Useful when you need to take your own work apart in words: ask it to describe
the composition, suggest what to improve, or write a prompt for the next
generation from it.

### Upscale resolution

Enlarges a finished picture without rebuilding it. The button opens a menu
with two items — **x2** and **x4**.

![The "Upscale resolution" hint](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-50g-upscale.en.webp)

![The upscale menu: x2 and x4](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-71-upscale-menu.en.webp)

The prompt plays no part here at all: upscaling works with pixels, not with
a description. The result arrives as a separate work.

### Upscale resolution x2 + prompt

The second upscale is always x2, with no choice of factor, but it **paints in
detail from your text**. It takes the prompt and "Negative" **from the fields
as they are right now**, not the ones the picture was made with.

![The "Upscale resolution x2 + prompt" hint](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-50f-upscale-prompt.en.webp)

With an empty prompt field the button will not fire and shows an error about
the missing prompt. That is the whole difference between the two: one simply
enlarges, the other enlarges and paints in detail from the description.

### Outpaint the image

Extends the frame beyond its borders: the service paints in what was never
in the shot. The button opens a menu of four sides — **top, left, right,
bottom**.

![The "Outpaint the image" hint](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-50h-outpaint.en.webp)

![The outpaint menu: top, left, right, bottom](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-72-outpaint-menu.en.webp)

Sides are picked one at a time, and each pass produces a new work. That is how
a tight composition turns into a wide shot: a few outpaints in a row and
a portrait becomes a scene. One pass adds roughly a quarter of the side, but
no more than a fixed maximum — which is why a wide shot is built in several
steps rather than one.

<Callout type="warning">
  **Outpainting has two limits, and both arrive as a message.** The same work
  cannot be outpainted forever: after a few passes the service says the limit
  is used up. And it will not outpaint a picture that is too large — it says
  that outright too.

  Hence the order of operations: **outpaint first, upscale afterwards.**
  An upscaled frame will almost certainly hit the size limit.
</Callout>

### Background remove

The only button with no menu: press it and the work goes into the queue
straight away. What comes back is the picture with a transparent background.

![The "Background remove" hint](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-50i-background-remove.en.webp)

### Copy Link

Puts the link to the work's page into the clipboard — the very page anyone
following it will see. It is a link to the page, not to the image file: next
to the work they will find its prompt, model and settings.

![The "Copy Link" hint](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-50j-copy-link.en.webp)

### Download

For pictures the button opens a menu of formats — **WebP, PNG and JPG**;
vector models add **SVG**, because the source itself is vector there.

![The "Download" hint](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-50k-download.en.webp)

![The download menu: WebP, PNG, JPG](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-73-download-formats.en.webp)

**Video and sound have no menu at all** — the file downloads at once, in one
press: a clip as MP4, a track in its own format. Raster formats do not apply
to them, so no choice is offered.

<Callout type="warning">
  **On the free plan downloading is available in WebP only**, PNG and JPG come
  with paid plans. And a separate case: a work marked 18+ will not download
  while adult content is switched off in profile settings — the settings open
  instead of the download.
</Callout>

### What is available on the free plan

| Action | Free | Any paid plan |
|---|---|---|
| Create a similar image | yes | yes |
| **Face restoration** | **yes** | yes |
| Copy link, download | yes | yes |
| Send to "Custom Image" | **no** | yes |
| Upscale and upscale with prompt | **no** | yes |
| Upscale together with face restoration | **no** | yes |
| Outpaint beyond the frame | **no** | yes |
| Background remove | **no** | yes |

<Callout type="tip">
  **Face restoration is the only enhancement available on the free plan.**
  Upscaling itself is not, and neither is "upscale together with face
  restoration": that is a separate action with its own restriction.
</Callout>

**A dimmed button and a highlighted one mean different things.** Dimmed means
there is nothing to work with: a generation is running, or there is no finished
result. Highlighted means the feature exists but is not part of your plan.
A **dimmed** button means something else: there is nothing to work with —
a generation is running or there is no finished result. Highlighted means
the plan is missing; pale means the occasion is.

While the buttons load, identical placeholders sit in their place: the panel does
not jump, but it does not work either — that is not a freeze. And if there is no
panel at all, there is no result: every button hides itself on an empty canvas.

## Video and sound

The "Video" and "Audio" tabs work the same way but with different settings.

![Generator, Video tab](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-42a-video-mode.en.webp)

Video brings duration, frames per second and resolution — from 480p up to 4K
on some models. **Video is charged per second**, so duration multiplies the price
directly.

![Video: 4K resolution, duration and keyframes](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-43-video-4k-duration.en.webp)

<Callout type="warning">
  **The list of durations depends on the chosen size.** On some models 4K offers
  one set of durations and 1080p another, and when you change the size
  an unsupported duration **moves itself** to the nearest allowed one. It looks
  like the interface acting on its own; in fact it is protection from a refusal.

  Hence the tip: if the duration you need is missing, try a different size.
  On a number of models long clips are only available at a lower resolution.
</Callout>

### What goes where in video

On video models files go into different slots rather than one, and this is
the main confusion of the section:

| Slot | What goes in |
|---|---|
| **Frames** | literally frames of your clip: the first, the last |
| **References (images)** | a sample of what it should look like: style, character |
| **References (video)** | movement or manner to be repeated |
| **References (audio)** | sound as a sample |
| **Audio** | the track that will be part of the clip itself |
| **Source video** | the clip you are editing (on editing models) |

Only the slots a model supports are shown — usually two or three, not all of
them. Each is a layer: it collapses, switches off and accepts drag-and-drop from
the left drawer.

<Callout type="warning">
  **The difference between "Frames" and "References (images)" is the commonest
  mistake.** A frame is literally a frame of the future clip — the model will
  start (or end) with it. A reference is only a sample to imitate; it will not
  appear in the clip.
</Callout>

Uploaded audio — **check the frames per second**: some models have their own
set of rates for a voiced clip, and the value may have jumped by itself. And
remember that on some models **the duration is taken from the length of
the track** — a one-minute audio gives a one-minute clip and a one-minute
price. Trimming the track beforehand is cheaper than generating spare seconds.

**"Extract a frame" takes neither the first nor the "best" frame, but the one
currently paused in the player.** So the order is: pause the clip at the moment
you want — and only then press the button. The frame is taken at the video's full
resolution, goes straight into "Custom Image" replacing the previous reference,
and the generator switches to the "Images" tab. If the frame could not be
captured, the clip's cover is used instead.

**A finished video has only three actions: extract a frame, copy the link and
download.** There is no upscaling, no outpainting and no "create similar"
for video — don't look for them. The only way to refine a clip: extract
a frame, fix it as a picture and animate it again.

![Generator, Audio tab](/assets/docs-pages/screens/05-46-audio-mode.en.webp)

Audio model tags split them into clear groups: **SPEECH** (voicing text),
**VOICE-CLONE** (voice cloning), **MUSIC** (music and songs), **SFX** (sound
effects). Voicing is charged **per character**, not per second — which is why
it has no duration slider.

<Callout type="warning">
  A finished track has **two** actions: copy the link and download. A track
  cannot be animated, sent to the chat or redone with one button — only
  by a new generation.
</Callout>

Both topics are covered in detail in
[Special model features](/en/docs/special).

## Resetting the settings

The **"Reset"** button next to "Create" does more than expected: it clears
**both the prompt and "Avoid"**, returns the model and every setting to their
defaults, and removes the previous result from the canvas. **There is no warning
beforehand** — you press it and it is done.

<Callout type="tip">
  Your works are **not deleted** — they stay in the feed on the left and in your
  profile; only the form is reset. But if the prompt matters, copy it first:
  it cannot be brought back afterwards. Or, instead of resetting, simply open any
  of your works from the drawer — its settings land on top of the current ones.
</Callout>

## What requires a paid plan

Some of the generator's features work on paid plans. If one of them is not open
to you right now, the service says so and names it: generating from your own
image and a mask, private mode, the "Masterpiece" creativity mode, premium
styles, voice input, prompt improvement, extracting a prompt from a picture,
extending the frame beyond its borders, background removal, upscaling together
with face restoration, and models above your plan.

No coins are charged for such a refusal. What exactly each plan includes is in
the plans window — see [Plans and payment](/en/docs/billing).

<Callout type="tip">
  Three of the messages are written in technical language and deserve
  a translation: **img2img** means generating from your own image, **outpaint**
  means extending the frame beyond its borders, and **prompt generation from
  an image** is that magnifier button which writes a prompt from a picture.
</Callout>

"Masterpiece" has an exception: on a few models it is open even on the free plan.
The rule is simple — if the mode button is active, this model is one of them.

## If the generation won't start

| What happened | What to do |
|---|---|
| **the prompt is shorter than five characters** | write more: the button stays dim while the text is too short |
| **the prompt is empty** and the model requires one | write something |
| **the visible area is not set** on the uploaded picture | press "Set visible area" and save |
| **the model requires an image or a mask** | add what is missing |
| **the "Custom Image" layer is switched off** | switch the layer on with the stack icon — the picture is loaded but not being sent |
| **a required field in "Additional" is empty** | look for the field with an asterisk — it is highlighted |
| **not enough coins** | claim the daily ones or top up — [Coins and limits](/en/docs/coins) |
| **the simultaneous generation limit is reached** | wait for one of the running ones to finish |
| **another generation is running** | the button does not dim, it spins — that is not a refusal |
| **no prompt entered** | "Upscale with prompt" does not work with an empty field |
| **the feature is not available on your plan** | see the section above and [Plans and payment](/en/docs/billing) |

<Callout type="warning">
  There is one more case: **a temporary block for repeated forbidden requests**.
  Send something the service refuses several times in a row and generation closes
  for a while — the message says how long is left. The block lifts by itself;
  there is no need to write to support, just rephrase the request.
</Callout>

On the free plan **an advertisement is shown in place of the empty canvas**.
It can be closed, but not instantly: a short countdown runs next to the cross.
After your first generation the canvas is taken by the work, and any paid plan
removes the advertising entirely — both here and in the feed.
