# Model catalogs

The catalog answers the question the generator makes you guess at: **which model
to take for this task**. Here every model has a description, a price, example
works and its neighbours in the line — all visible before you spend any coins.

![Image model catalog](/assets/docs-pages/screens/08-01-models-catalog.en.webp)

The tabs at the top: **Images · Video · Audio · Text · LoRAs · Styles**, each
with a number in brackets.

<Callout type="warning">
  **The catalog shows models with your plan in mind.** It is not a general price
  list but what applies to you. A modality can also be in a "Coming soon" state —
  the tab is there but the catalog is replaced by a placeholder; that is not
  an error.
</Callout>

## Filters: the left column

At the top there are three separate rows: **bookmarked only** (with a star),
**new only** (red tag), **hits only** (orange). Below them, collapsible sections:
**Category**, **Tariff**, **Family**, **Provider**.

![Video model catalog](/assets/docs-pages/screens/08-01b-catalog-video.en.webp)

<Callout type="tip">
  **Start with categories, not families.** Categories come first on purpose:
  that is how you search "by task". Family helps only once you already know
  the name of the line.
</Callout>

**The number to the right of each item does not change when you tick other
filters** — it is always about the full catalog. Only the "selected / total"
counter above the grid changes. This is often read as "the filter is broken".

A few more details that save time:

- **items with zero are not listed** — if something is missing, models like that
  simply do not exist right now;
- inside a section items are ordered **by number of models**, not alphabetically;
- the **"Reset"** link appears only when something is selected;
- all filters work **on the already loaded list**, so the counter updates
  instantly.

<Callout type="tip">
  **The "Tariff" filter shows at once how
  many models are available to you and how many would open on a paid plan.
  That way you won't pick a model you cannot run.
</Callout>

## Sorting

Five options: **default**, new, cheaper, more expensive, by name.

<Callout type="warning">
  "Default" is **the order recommended by the service**, not popularity among
  users. It should not be read as a ranking.
</Callout>

**"Cheaper" plus a category is the most practical trick in the catalog.**
The price spread is enormous: half of the image models cost a token amount,
while the flagships are hundreds of times more. For drafts and for tuning
a prompt, a cheap model works just as well.

## The model card

![Audio model catalog](/assets/docs-pages/screens/08-01c-catalog-audio.en.webp)

The card's cover is **a real work made on that model**. The whole card leads
to the model page, and the star in the corner is a bookmark: clicking it does
not open the page.

<Callout type="warning">
  **The same number on cards means different things:**

  - **images** — the price per picture;
  - **video** — **"from N"**: the minimum cost of a clip, not the price per
    second;
  - **audio** — either "from N" for a minimal track, or **"N / 1K"** for models
    that count by characters;
  - **text** — **"N / 1000 tokens"**.

  Look not at the number itself but at the caption next to it: "from", "/ 1K"
  and "/ 1000 tokens" change the meaning of the price completely.
</Callout>

![Text model catalog](/assets/docs-pages/screens/08-01d-catalog-text.en.webp)

### What NEW and HIT mean

- **NEW** — the model appeared recently; the tag **drops off by itself** after
  about a month. It really does mean "new", not "we feel like showing this".
- **HIT** — the model is currently among the most used, with a window of a couple
  of working days. The set of models carrying it **changes on its own**.

<Callout type="tip">
  Hence an honest piece of advice: **not sure where to start — take a model
  tagged HIT**. That is not an editorial pick but what everyone else is using
  right now. Both tags can be topped up manually by the team, so "strictly
  top N" cannot be promised.
</Callout>

**The plan badge appears on a card only when the model is above your plan.**
So **a missing badge is good news**: the model is available to you. A guest
sees cards with no badges at all, and that does not mean everything is free.

## The model page

![Model page](/assets/docs-pages/screens/08-04-model-page.en.webp)

Top to bottom: breadcrumbs, a large preview, the name, tags and price,
the description, **two price tables** (by size and by mode), the **"Try model"**
button, **example works** and **similar models**.

<Callout type="tip">
  **"Try model" takes you straight to the right place** — the generator with that
  model, or the chat if the model is a text one. No need to pick it again.
</Callout>

### Example works

The examples are **real works made on that model**, not marketing pictures.

<Callout type="tip">
  **Clicking an example opens the work card right there**, without taking you to
  the gallery. So from a model page you can look at an example's prompt and
  settings and take them for yourself — see [Gallery](/en/docs/gallery).
</Callout>

Two rows are shown; the "Show all" button appears only if there are more examples
than fit. If there are none at all, there is no block — that is not a loading
error.

### Similar models

Picked automatically: the strongest factor is **the same family**, then a shared
word in the name, then shared categories. No more than eight are shown.

<Callout type="tip">
  **A video model can appear among the "similar" ones for an image model — and
  that is not a fault.** It is the easiest way
  to discover that your favourite image line has a video relative. There is no
  separate switch for that anywhere.

  Liked the result of a particular model — scroll its page down to "Similar":
  there you will find both a cheaper stand-in from the same line and its version
  for another media type.
</Callout>

## Families: what that word in the filters means

A family is **a line from one developer**: it has a shared character, shared
strengths and, as a rule, shared LoRAs.

<Callout type="tip">
  **Remember the family, not just the model name.** Neighbours in a line behave
  similarly, and the same LoRAs will fit them.
</Callout>

Two patterns worth knowing:

- **the catalog is lopsided**: the largest family holds more than half of all
  image models, while many others have one or two. That is why the "Family"
  filter helps when you already know the line;
- **price is strongly tied to the family**: large open lines are the cheapest,
  branded models from big companies are the most expensive, with a gap
  of hundreds of times. It is the only clear pattern in the pricing.

<Callout type="warning">
  The **"Provider"** filter gives almost nothing for images right now — there is
  a single provider. Not by accident is that section collapsed by default.
</Callout>

## Why one model has a pile of settings and another none

The "Additional" block in the generator is far from universal: roughly one image
model in seven has it, four out of five video models do, and **audio and text
models all do**.

<Callout type="tip">
  This is the direct answer to "why is 'Additional' empty on my image model":
  that is how it is for most of them. In video and audio, on the other hand,
  there is always something there. More about settings —
  [Generator](/en/docs/generator).
</Callout>

## The LoRA catalog

![LoRA catalog](/assets/docs-pages/screens/08-05-loras-catalog.en.webp)

A LoRA is **an add-on to a model**, not a model of its own: it mixes in
a particular style, character or technique.

A LoRA's category answers the question "what does it do": style, character,
concept, object, pose, tool, photorealism, anime, illustration. **More than half
of all LoRAs are styles**, so if you are after "make it look good", that is where
to go; "a specific character" is a different and much smaller category.

<Callout type="warning">
  **The LoRA catalog and the LoRA panel in the generator have different filters —
  they are two different places.** Here in the catalog you can filter by
  **family** as well as by category. In the generator there is only one
  attribute — category — and several selected ones combine with "AND".

  Hence the best trick on this page: **pick your model's family in the catalog and
  you will see only the LoRAs that fit it**. There is no such filter in
  the generator; looking for it there is pointless.
</Callout>

![LoRA page](/assets/docs-pages/screens/08-06-lora-page.en.webp)

The LoRA page has things its card inside the generator does not:

- **"Trigger"** — the activator word with a copy button;
- **"Default weight"** — the strength recommended by the author. Don't guess,
  start from it;
- **"Try in the generator"** and a **"Similar LoRAs"** block.

<Callout type="tip">
  **Not every LoRA has a "Trigger" block.** If no activator word is set, there
  simply is no block — then look at the description, it is often mentioned there
  as plain text.

  And the main thing: **activator words are appended to the prompt
  automatically**. The trigger on the page is a reference — "this is what will be
  added to your prompt" — not a task to type it yourself.
</Callout>

**"Try in the generator" does more than it looks.** The button does not just
open the generator: it substitutes a suitable model from the same family and
**selects that LoRA straight away**, expanding the LoRA section. Two clicks
instead of "pick a model → find the LoRA in the list → switch it on".

The sign of success is the LoRA section opening by itself. If it didn't,
the LoRA is not compatible with the substituted model and the generator opened
without it.

### Which models have LoRAs and which don't

About four out of five image models support LoRAs, but they are spread very
unevenly:

- **open "community" lines have LoRAs for practically every model** — hundreds
  of them;
- **branded models from big companies have none at all**.

<Callout type="tip">
  A ready rule for choosing: need a specific character, a drawing style or a rare
  technique — take a model from a line where LoRAs live. Need "just a good result
  from a description" — branded models do fine without them.
</Callout>

**The same family does not yet mean compatibility.** Within one line name there
are models of different technical generations, and a LoRA from one will not fit
another. Checking is simple: pick the model — the LoRA section either has
a list or is empty.

## The style catalog

![Style catalog](/assets/docs-pages/screens/08-07-styles-catalog.en.webp)

A style is a ready-made add-on to your prompt: it appends wording that sets
the manner.

![Style page](/assets/docs-pages/screens/08-08-style-page.en.webp)

A style's page carries a **"Prompt template"** and a **"Negative prompt"**. The
word "template" is literal here: it is a frame **your prompt is inserted into** —
which is why it is worth reading before you write your own text.

<Callout type="tip">
  Like LoRAs, a style has a "Try" button. You can select **no more than three**
  styles and three LoRAs at once — and all of it counts towards the prompt length.
</Callout>

**Why one model has few styles and another many.** Styles are picked by
a ladder, and **the first non-empty level wins**: first the styles tied to
the model itself; if there are none — the styles of its family; then those of
its provider; and only if all of that is empty — the general ones.

Hence the key consequence: **if a model has its own styles, the general ones
are no longer shown to it**. A short list is not poverty but a precise
selection for that model. And conversely, identical long lists across different
models are the general styles inherited by default.

The practical upshot: **don't hunt for "the right style" by cycling through
models**. Pick the model first and then look at what is offered for it —
the list is already filtered.

## Bookmarks

The star exists for models, LoRAs and styles. Bookmarks are **stored in your
account**, not in the browser, so they are available from any device — and they
work only for signed-in users.

<Callout type="tip">
  These are the very same bookmarks that the **"bookmarked only"** switch in
  the generator turns on. Star a model in the catalog and it will rise in
  the list there too.
</Callout>
